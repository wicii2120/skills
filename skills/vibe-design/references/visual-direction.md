# Visual Direction & Taste

Load this for concrete visual work, aesthetic intent, or craft review. It supplies contextual judgment, not a house style.

## 1. Ground the direction

Read the artifact before choosing an aesthetic. Ground decisions in:

- subject and its real materials, tools, artifacts, vocabulary, and emotional register;
- audience, trust level, expertise, ability, and cultural/localization context;
- primary task and frequency of use;
- real content and asset quality;
- validated brand, product, and platform conventions;
- implementation capacity, performance envelope, and lifecycle.

State one line before concrete design:

> **Design Read:** `[artifact/page kind] for [audience/task], using [visual/verbal/interaction language] because [evidence], with [system/platform] constraints.`

When evidence is absent, mark the read as an assumption. A mood word alone is not evidence.

## 2. Declare three intent dimensions

Use these dimensions when they sharpen decisions. A numeric scale is optional; any value is a contextual starting point, never a universal default.

| Dimension | Low end | High end | Decisions affected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Design variance** | Familiar, symmetric, predictable | Asymmetric, surprising, composition-led | Grid, hierarchy, shape, overlap, navigation familiarity |
| **Motion intensity** | Static/instant state feedback | Choreographed, physical, explanatory | Frequency gate, purpose, mechanism, duration/spring, reduced equivalent |
| **Visual density** | Airy, few simultaneous elements | Compact, information-rich | Type scale, spacing, grouping, disclosure, targets, responsive priority |

Record why each position suits the task. A regulated daily tool may need low variance and motion with medium-high density; a rare brand moment may justify more expression. The same dial label does not imply the same implementation across platforms.

## 3. Build a coherent direction

- **Opening composition:** treat a hero or opening region as the artifact’s thesis when one exists. Lead with the most characteristic content, interaction, image, or claim rather than a generic statistic/card formula.
- **Typography:** use type for personality and hierarchy as well as delivery. Choose roles, contrast, and optical treatment from the subject, brand, content, script, and loading constraints.
- **Structure as information:** numbering, eyebrows, dividers, cards, bento grids, split layouts, and labels encode real grouping or sequence. Decoration that implies false structure is noise.
- **Materiality and shape:** establish a coherent surface, radius, border, shadow, texture, and light logic. Variation follows a rule rather than component-by-component taste.
- **Visual rhythm:** vary scale, spacing, repetition, and interruption to support reading flow. Uniform slabs feel templated; arbitrary rhythm feels unstable.
- **Composition and density:** align the number of simultaneous elements with task complexity and attention. Responsive behavior preserves hierarchy, not only geometry.
- **Signature decision:** make one deliberate identity-bearing choice. It may be subtle, structural, typographic, spatial, verbal, visual, or interactive. Spend boldness there and keep supporting decisions disciplined.
- **Copy as material:** headings, labels, examples, status, and errors shape composition and trust. Design with real content before filler.
- **Complexity fit:** maximal directions demand implementation depth; minimal directions demand precision. Choose a direction the available team, stack, assets, and verification can execute completely.
- **Mode scope:** light/dark is a product/system decision. A named theme may be light, dark, adaptive, or mode-independent; never add dark mode solely as a taste signal.

## 4. Choose from contextual aesthetic families

Aesthetic families are exploration vocabulary, not packages or default recipes. Examples include editorial, utilitarian, industrial/brutalist, soft/material, restrained minimal, kinetic/expressive, playful, archival, technical, luxury, or platform-native. Combine only when the concept explains the seam.

For a named theme, define its subject/audience fit, semantic palette roles, type roles, shape/material rules, imagery, motion personality, density, responsive behavior, accessibility, and anti-patterns. Theme names describe the concept rather than claiming a universal best use.

## 5. Use the AI-default smell catalog

Treat these as prompts to ask “why,” not hard bans:

- a familiar default font or arbitrary display/body pairing;
- an ungrounded purple/blue glow, warm craft palette, or mode inversion;
- centered hero plus three equal cards, repetitive card rows, empty bento cells, or endless split-section zigzags;
- eyebrow labels, version tags, pills, dividers, decorative status dots, or numbered sections without semantic purpose;
- punctuation, sentence rhythms, poetic microcopy, or fake precision that reads generated rather than product-specific;
- uniform radii, shadows, glass, gradient text, fake product screenshots, and floating decoration repeated by habit;
- motion because an engine is available, ambient loops everywhere, or “static is forbidden” reasoning;
- stock claims, generic names, placeholder logos, fabricated endorsements, or visual proof of a product that does not exist.

A familiar pattern is acceptable when product evidence, platform convention, task frequency, or brand supports it. Novelty is not valuable when arbitrary. Source-specific font, palette, punctuation, hero, bento, and motion rules remain catalog entries unless adopted as product policy.

## 6. Run the craft review

Every concrete craft review applies:

1. **Grounding test:** Which decisions derive from audience, task, content, brand, subject, or platform? Mark unsupported decisions.
2. **Default test:** Which choices appear selected from habit, generator tropes, or an unrelated prior project? Replace only those without evidence.
3. **Removal test:** Remove at least one accessory, effect, label, animation, or surface that carries no meaning.

Then inspect typography, hierarchy, composition, rhythm, material/shape logic, content, imagery, state quality, motion, responsive hierarchy, and implementation fidelity against the Design Read.

## 7. Classify recommendations

Label recommendations so preference cannot masquerade as law:

- **Normative requirement** — accessibility, platform, legal, or license obligation.
- **Product constraint** — explicit brief, brand, content, technical, performance, or organizational requirement.
- **Governed system rule** — adopted token, component, pattern, or policy.
- **Contextual craft heuristic** — evidence-informed judgment for this artifact.
- **Exploration option** — plausible alternative requiring comparison or validation.

**Completion:** The direction has a Design Read, justified intent dimensions, one coherent signature, classified decisions, an anti-default review, and evidence that the final composition still serves task, content, accessibility, and implementation capacity.
