# Visual Style Lexicon

Load this only for a named style, style comparison or explanation, style-led exploration, or unresolved visual direction where named alternatives reduce decision risk. It is contextual exploration vocabulary, not a preset library or a source of defaults.

A canonical name can act as a **leading word**: published vision-language and image-model research makes learned label-to-visual associations plausible, but no located benchmark isolates canonical-style fidelity in coding-agent UI generation. Recognition varies by model and context; no claim about any model's training data follows. The cards therefore translate names into observable constraints instead of relying on the name alone. See [Provenance](provenance.md#visual-style-lexicon-research).

For visual recognition, comparison, teaching, or image-grounded direction work on a retained anchor, load the [Visual Style Examples](visual-style-examples.md) catalog. It is reference evidence only: it adds no anchors, changes no definitions, and grants no production rights.

## 1. Select and translate

1. Begin with the [Design Read](visual-direction.md#1-ground-the-direction). A style name never substitutes for audience, task, content, brand, or platform evidence.
2. Classify each requested term as a **style anchor**, **modifier**, **technique/pattern**, **broader family**, or **branded system**. Clarify only when ambiguity would change the result.
3. Choose one primary style anchor. Add at most one compatible modifier when it sharpens the concept; a mood, period, material, or tone does not become a second anchor.
4. Translate the anchor and modifier into observable decisions through the applicable foundations: [Typography](../foundations/typography.md), [Color](../foundations/color-system.md), [Grid & Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md), [Spacing](../foundations/spacing.md), [Imagery](../foundations/imagery.md), [Iconography](../foundations/iconography.md), [Motion & Interaction](../foundations/motion-interaction.md), and [Content & UX Writing](../foundations/content-writing.md). Those files remain authoritative for values, semantics, accessibility, and implementation.
5. Reconcile the translation with real assets, adopted tokens/components, brand, performance, localization, responsive behavior, and platform conventions. Normative accessibility and official platform contracts take precedence.
6. If two anchors are explicitly combined, state the **conceptual seam**: which anchor owns which dimensions, why they belong together, and how conflicts resolve. Use `A owns [dimensions]; B owns [dimensions]; [product/platform rule] wins on [conflict]`. Two styles cannot silently own the same decision.
7. Verify the result against the Design Read, the card's boundary and false friends, the visual-direction craft review, and rendered evidence when available. Familiar patterns remain valid when evidence supports them.

For comparison, hold the product decision constant; state shared lineage, then distinguish the styles on at least three defining dimensions, fit, and risks. For explanation, give the canonical name and aliases once, then translate it into a reusable constraint statement.

**Anchor inclusion gate:** retain a card only when the term has (1) a canonical stable English name and coherent core, (2) reliable semantic support—two independent sources for material historical claims where practical or a first-party source for a branded system, (3) distinctions from adjacent terms on at least three relevant dimensions, (4) enough scope to guide an interface rather than only a mood, effect, motif, technique, layout, or product category, and (5) a translation that preserves usability, accessibility, brand, platform, and implementation constraints. Reclassify rather than pad when any condition fails.

## 3. Historical movements and graphic languages

### Arts and Crafts

- **Aliases · category:** Arts & Crafts · historical design movement.
- **Core · boundary:** Integrity of making, visible material character, and integrated craft across type, image, pattern, and object. Unlike Art Nouveau, it centers workmanship and vernacular structure rather than the flowing line itself.
- **Defining cues:** Human-scaled composition; crafted or calligraphic display details; natural or medieval-derived pattern; tactile illustration and material texture; sturdy rather than sleek rhythm.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits cultural, maker, heritage, food, publishing, and place-based products with real craft evidence; mismatches high-frequency dense tools when ornament or bespoke assets slow scanning.
- **Risks:** Asset cost, font licensing, texture weight, poor small-size detail, and romanticizing labor or heritage without evidence.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** A paper texture and botanical border do not make the movement. Preserve construction logic and provenance; avoid generic cottagecore.
- **Translation:** Let one genuinely sourced craft process shape display type, illustration, and section rhythm; keep task controls conventional, body content quiet, and decorative assets bounded by loading and contrast budgets.
- **Foundations:** [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Imagery](../foundations/imagery.md) · [Content](../foundations/content-writing.md). **Evidence:** VH1.

### Art Nouveau

- **Aliases · category:** Jugendstil, Sezessionstil, Modern Style; clarify regional variant only when material · historical international design style.
- **Core · boundary:** Structure and ornament unified through sinuous or regionally geometric forms derived from nature. Unlike Arts and Crafts, the characteristic line and total decorative composition lead; unlike Art Deco, geometry is less machine-precise and symmetry less dominant.
- **Defining cues:** Whiplash curves or disciplined organic geometry; asymmetrical framing; integrated lettering and ornament; botanical, insect, or flowing-figure imagery; continuous edge-to-content relationships.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits cultural events, beauty, hospitality, botanical subjects, and bounded brand moments; mismatches dense operational screens or content that lacks credible natural/craft connection.
- **Risks:** Decorative text loses legibility, frames compete with controls, detailed assets hurt performance, and historical motifs become cultural costume.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** Filigree or flowers alone are techniques. Use a coherent line and composition system rather than pasting motifs onto a generic card layout.
- **Translation:** Establish one organic line rule that connects framing, crop, and display lettering; reserve it for hierarchy-bearing regions while body text, forms, focus, and responsive flow remain plain and robust.
- **Foundations:** [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Imagery](../foundations/imagery.md) · [Icons](../foundations/iconography.md). **Evidence:** VH2, TH1.

### Art Deco

- **Aliases · category:** Deco · historical decorative style.
- **Core · boundary:** Machine-age glamour expressed through ordered geometry, stylized ornament, and crafted finish. Unlike Art Nouveau's organic line, Deco favors stepped, radial, streamlined, or faceted structure; luxury is a possible modifier, not its definition.
- **Defining cues:** Symmetry or axial emphasis; geometric display lettering; stepped, fan, ray, or faceted forms; high material contrast; stylized rather than naturalistic imagery.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits hospitality, culture, entertainment, ceremonial commerce, and historically grounded brands; mismatches routine administration or urgent transactional flows when spectacle delays action.
- **Risks:** Display-type legibility, visual weight, decorative asset cost, clichéd metallic treatment, and overstatement of exclusivity.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** A dark field, gold accent, or sunburst is not a complete direction. Preserve disciplined proportion and repeat only a small, meaningful geometric vocabulary.
- **Translation:** Use axial hierarchy, measured geometric repetition, and one high-craft display moment; derive colors and materials from brand evidence, and keep data, forms, and states free of ornamental interference.
- **Foundations:** [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Color](../foundations/color-system.md) · [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Imagery](../foundations/imagery.md). **Evidence:** VH3.

### Bauhaus

- **Aliases · category:** Bauhaus style · historical school and design movement.
- **Core · boundary:** Art, craft, and industrial production organized around function, elemental form, and teachable systems. Unlike Swiss Style, it is not chiefly a neutral typographic grid; unlike De Stijl, it is not limited to orthogonal planes.
- **Defining cues:** Elemental geometry; asymmetric functional composition; direct sans-serif or constructed display type; economical color relationships; visible correspondence between form and purpose.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits education, culture, tools, product launches, and systems whose structure can become identity; mismatches requests seeking ornamental nostalgia without a functional seam.
- **Risks:** Primary-shape cliché, oversimplified hierarchy, geometric decoration that implies false grouping, and inaccessible color adjacency.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** Circles, triangles, and primary hues alone are "Bauhaus cosplay." Every shape must carry hierarchy, interaction, or subject meaning.
- **Translation:** Build an asymmetric modular composition in which type, geometry, and interaction share one rule; use only brand-compatible accents that carry semantic roles, and keep familiar controls recognizable.
- **Foundations:** [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Color](../foundations/color-system.md) · [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Spacing](../foundations/spacing.md). **Evidence:** BH1, TH1.

### International Typographic Style

- **Aliases · category:** Swiss Style, Swiss typography · historical graphic-design movement.
- **Core · boundary:** Clear, ostensibly objective communication through a rigorous grid, asymmetric hierarchy, and disciplined sans-serif typography. Unlike Minimalism, reduction serves information organization rather than element count alone; unlike Bauhaus, expressive geometry is secondary.
- **Defining cues:** Modular grid; flush-left hierarchy with controlled rag; few typographic roles and strong scale contrast; generous negative space; documentary photography or restrained graphic matter.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits information services, portfolios, institutions, dashboards, and multilingual systems with stable content structure; mismatches concepts that depend on tactile ornament or intentionally unstable narrative flow.
- **Risks:** Sterility, over-wide desktop grids, weak mobile reflow, overreliance on one typeface, and treating neutrality as culturally universal.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** Any clean sans-serif page is not Swiss Style. The grid, alignment, measure, and information hierarchy must work together; Helvetica is never mandatory.
- **Translation:** Define a content-derived modular grid and a small role-based type system; let alignment and scale carry hierarchy, constrain accents to semantic jobs, and preserve source order through reflow.
- **Foundations:** [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Spacing](../foundations/spacing.md) · [Imagery](../foundations/imagery.md). **Evidence:** SG1.

### Constructivism

- **Aliases · category:** Russian Constructivism · historical movement.
- **Core · boundary:** Art organized as active construction for modern communication, often using forceful geometry, diagonals, photomontage, and compressed public-message hierarchy. Unlike Futurism, it tends toward engineered assembly and declarative communication rather than continuous motion.
- **Defining cues:** Diagonal or radial composition; bold geometric type; tight high-contrast palette relationships; photomontage or cropped documentary imagery; strong scale jumps and directional bars.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits campaigns, cultural programming, editorial openings, and civic calls to action with a justified urgent voice; mismatches calm trust-sensitive tasks or long reading when intensity is unbounded.
- **Risks:** Rotated-text legibility, propaganda associations, visual aggression, non-Latin-script mishandling, and collage licensing.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** Red, black, and a diagonal do not establish the style. Avoid pseudo-Cyrillic lettering and ideological symbols as decoration.
- **Translation:** Use one directional axis and a compressed type-image hierarchy to express a real sequence or call to action; keep body reading, controls, and recovery states on a stable orthogonal layer.
- **Foundations:** [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Color](../foundations/color-system.md) · [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Imagery](../foundations/imagery.md). **Evidence:** TH1.

### De Stijl

- **Aliases · category:** Neoplasticism; use "Mondrian-like" only as an informal visual reference, not the canonical term · historical movement.
- **Core · boundary:** Asymmetric equilibrium reduced to orthogonal relations, open fields, and discrete planes. Unlike Bauhaus, the grammar is narrower and planar; unlike a bento layout, divisions form a total compositional system rather than a card arrangement.
- **Defining cues:** Horizontal and vertical structure; strong line/field relationships; flat bounded planes; asymmetric balance; highly constrained color roles.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits portfolios, cultural storytelling, spatial navigation, and products with a small number of strong regions; mismatches dense forms or tables when the compositional grid competes with data structure.
- **Risks:** False grouping, excessive borders, fragile narrow-screen crops, palette caricature, and controls mistaken for composition blocks.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** A Mondrian grid is a technique, not proof of the movement. Do not force every component into colored rectangles.
- **Translation:** Assign a few content regions to an orthogonal field system with deliberate unequal weight; preserve ordinary component semantics inside those regions and collapse the composition to a clear source-ordered stack.
- **Foundations:** [Color](../foundations/color-system.md) · [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Spacing](../foundations/spacing.md) · [Content](../foundations/content-writing.md). **Evidence:** TH1.

### Futurism

- **Aliases · category:** Italian Futurism when historical specificity matters · historical movement.
- **Core · boundary:** Speed, energy, machinery, and simultaneity made visible through directional repetition and fractured movement. Unlike Constructivism's assembled public-message grammar, Futurism centers perceived motion and temporal force.
- **Defining cues:** Repeated or radiating forms; oblique composition; compressed kinetic lettering; mechanical imagery; motion trails, sequencing, or rhythmic interruption.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits mobility, sport, performance, experimental media, and bounded launch moments; mismatches frequent-use tools, safety-critical tasks, or calm reading.
- **Risks:** Motion sensitivity, reading-order disruption, performance cost, aggressive tone, and the movement's politically loaded history.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** Italics and speed lines are not enough. Borrow observable formal devices without importing ideology, slogans, or historical symbols.
- **Translation:** Let one real action or sequence determine the directional rhythm; provide a stable reading layer and reduced-motion equivalent, and keep task completion independent of animation.
- **Foundations:** [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Motion](../foundations/motion-interaction.md) · [Imagery](../foundations/imagery.md). **Evidence:** TH1.

### Pop Art

- **Aliases · category:** Pop · historical art and graphic vernacular.
- **Core · boundary:** Mass-media, commercial, comic, and consumer imagery reframed through repetition, enlargement, and sharp graphic contrast. Unlike Maximalism, it has a specific popular-media subject language; unlike Memphis, it does not depend on decorative pattern clash.
- **Defining cues:** Cropped or repeated familiar imagery; flat high-impact color relationships; bold display type; halftone or print-production texture; scale shifts between ordinary objects and headline forms.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits entertainment, campaigns, commerce, and cultural critique with suitable content; mismatches solemn services or interfaces where visual quotation obscures trust.
- **Risks:** Copyright, trademark and likeness rights; visual noise; small-text contrast; and confusing parody with endorsement.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** Halftone dots and speech bubbles are techniques. Use real subject matter and a clear stance rather than copying a recognizable artist's work.
- **Translation:** Choose one product-relevant everyday image or phrase for controlled repetition or enlargement; keep navigation, prices, states, and legal content on a quieter layer with verified rights.
- **Foundations:** [Color](../foundations/color-system.md) · [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Imagery](../foundations/imagery.md) · [Content](../foundations/content-writing.md). **Evidence:** TH1.

### Memphis

- **Aliases · category:** Memphis Group, Memphis Milano; "postmodern" is the broader family · historical postmodern design movement.
- **Core · boundary:** Deliberate opposition to sober functionalism through playful clash, mixed references, graphic pattern, and object-like geometry. Unlike New Wave Typography, the style is not primarily a typographic critique; unlike generic playful design, tension and juxtaposition are structural.
- **Defining cues:** Asymmetric object-like composition; clashing pattern and shape families; unexpected material/color relationships; mixed-scale display type; humorous visual interruption.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits expressive retail, culture, children or youth brands, and campaigns with appetite for deliberate tension; mismatches dense enterprise, healthcare, or high-consequence tasks when expression dominates clarity.
- **Risks:** Sensory overload, localization expansion, inconsistent controls, animation excess, and trend-costume nostalgia.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** Squiggles, confetti, and terrazzo are motifs. Use a governed clash rule and do not label all 1980s geometry "Memphis."
- **Translation:** Let a small set of contrasting shapes and patterns own framing and brand moments while one stable grid, type hierarchy, and component language own tasks and states.
- **Foundations:** [Color](../foundations/color-system.md) · [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Motion](../foundations/motion-interaction.md). **Evidence:** ME1, TH1.

### New Wave Typography

- **Aliases · category:** Swiss Punk, postmodern typography; "New Wave" alone is ambiguous · historical graphic language.
- **Core · boundary:** Swiss typographic rigor questioned from within through broken grids, layered image and type, variable spacing, and deliberate tension. Unlike Swiss Style, order becomes visible by being disrupted; unlike Memphis, typography and reproduction process lead.
- **Defining cues:** Offset or stepped text blocks; elastic or broken grid; extreme tracking/scale changes; overlapping image, rule, and type; visible reproduction texture.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits culture, music, editorial features, portfolios, and experimental brand moments; mismatches long transactional copy, localization-heavy forms, or accessibility-critical instructions when disruption reaches the reading layer.
- **Risks:** Reading-order ambiguity, poor measure, text-as-image, zoom/reflow failure, and imitation of degraded print without purpose.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** Random rotation and overlap are not New Wave. The composition should reveal mastery of the grid it bends.
- **Translation:** Keep semantic order and body reading stable, then let one display layer break alignment through controlled spacing, overlap, or scale; define exactly where the grid resumes.
- **Foundations:** [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Imagery](../foundations/imagery.md) · [Content](../foundations/content-writing.md). **Evidence:** SG1.

## 4. Interface paradigms and web vernaculars

### Minimalism

- **Aliases · category:** Minimalist interface design · interface paradigm with art/design lineage.
- **Core · boundary:** Remove content and elements that do not support the task, then make the remaining hierarchy precise. Unlike Flat Design, it governs feature, content, and composition reduction rather than surface rendering alone; unlike Swiss Style, it does not require a particular grid grammar.
- **Defining cues:** Few simultaneous elements; strong negative space; limited visual roles; typography-led hierarchy; restrained surface and imagery count.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits focused products, reading, premium restraint, and simple tasks; mismatches complex workflows when removal hides necessary context or actions.
- **Risks:** Low information scent, hidden navigation, ghost controls, low-contrast text, over-large whitespace, and confusing emptiness with calm.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** White space, monochrome color, or a sparse landing page alone are not minimalism. Every omission needs a task rationale.
- **Translation:** Remove unsupported elements before styling; make the remaining primary action, hierarchy, state, and navigation explicit, then use measured space and a small number of roles without weakening signifiers.
- **Foundations:** [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Spacing](../foundations/spacing.md) · [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Content](../foundations/content-writing.md). **Evidence:** UX1, TH1, MX1.

### Maximalism

- **Aliases · category:** Maximalist design · visual strategy and stable vernacular.
- **Core · boundary:** Deliberate abundance organized through hierarchy, repetition, and contrast. Unlike clutter, density is composed; unlike Memphis, no single historical pattern/shape language is required.
- **Defining cues:** Multiple scales and focal layers; ornament- or image-positive surfaces; strong color/type contrast; dense rhythm with deliberate pauses; repeated motifs that unify variety.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits entertainment, fashion, culture, collections, and expressive commerce with strong assets; mismatches safety-critical flows or teams unable to produce and test the required depth.
- **Risks:** Cognitive overload, slow rendering, localization collisions, weak focus, motion excess, and inaccessible text over imagery.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** More cards, colors, and effects do not make maximalism. One hierarchy and repetition rule must make abundance navigable.
- **Translation:** Define one dominant focal layer, one recurring motif, and clear quiet zones; cap simultaneous motion, keep task surfaces stable, and budget every asset and contrast relationship.
- **Foundations:** [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Color](../foundations/color-system.md) · [Imagery](../foundations/imagery.md) · [Motion](../foundations/motion-interaction.md). **Evidence:** MX1; UX1 supplies the adjacent minimalist boundary, not a maximalist UI benchmark.

### Skeuomorphism

- **Aliases · category:** Skeuomorphic design · interface paradigm.
- **Core · boundary:** A digital object or interaction borrows a physical precedent to transfer a useful mental model. Realism that only imitates texture is a false friend; functional correspondence distinguishes skeuomorphism.
- **Defining cues:** Recognizable object metaphors; material texture and depth tied to interaction; physical control/state analogies; illustrative iconography or chrome.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits unfamiliar tools, simulations, creative instruments, and domains with a strong physical referent; mismatches workflows where the metaphor constrains efficient digital behavior.
- **Risks:** Visual noise, dated assets, ambiguous metaphor across cultures, heavy rendering, and physical constraints copied where they harm usability.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** Leather, wood, gloss, and shadows alone are realism. Keep only analogies that improve prediction or learning.
- **Translation:** Name the physical model and the user inference it should enable; represent only those material cues needed for affordance or state, while allowing efficient digital shortcuts and accessible semantics.
- **Foundations:** [Imagery](../foundations/imagery.md) · [Icons](../foundations/iconography.md) · [Motion](../foundations/motion-interaction.md) · [Content](../foundations/content-writing.md). **Evidence:** UX1.

### Flat Design / Flat 2.0

- **Aliases · category:** Flat UI; semi-flat and almost-flat are aliases for Flat 2.0 · interface rendering paradigm.
- **Core · boundary:** Flat Design removes realistic texture and obvious three-dimensional effects; Flat 2.0 restores selective depth and other signifiers where they communicate interaction or hierarchy. It can be visually rich and is not synonymous with Minimalism.
- **Defining cues:** Solid planes; simple icon and shape construction; little or no decorative texture; direct typography; in Flat 2.0, restrained layering, borders, or shadows with semantic purpose.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits broad product UI and scalable systems; fully flat treatment mismatches unfamiliar or high-consequence interactions when users need stronger signifiers.
- **Risks:** Click uncertainty, indistinguishable controls and labels, state ambiguity, icon dependence, and thin low-contrast boundaries.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** Removing every shadow or border is not digital purity. Flat 2.0 is a usability correction, not a decorative long-shadow effect.
- **Translation:** Keep surfaces visually economical, then add the minimum redundant cues needed to identify controls, focus, selection, layering, and state across input modes.
- **Foundations:** [Color](../foundations/color-system.md) · [Icons](../foundations/iconography.md) · [Motion](../foundations/motion-interaction.md) · [Content](../foundations/content-writing.md). **Evidence:** UX1.

### Glassmorphism

- **Aliases · category:** Glass UI, frosted-glass UI; Apple Liquid Glass and Microsoft Acrylic are governed implementations, not aliases · interface material style.
- **Core · boundary:** Translucent, blurred surfaces establish depth while retaining a controlled trace of the background. Unlike Neumorphism, layers are separated by transmission and blur rather than same-color embossing.
- **Defining cues:** Background blur plus translucency; bounded layered depth; edge contrast or subtle material boundary; restrained use over controlled backgrounds.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits overlays, navigation, media, spatial computing, and selected summary surfaces; mismatches dense analytics or reading surfaces when changing backgrounds undermine contrast.
- **Risks:** Contrast changes across content, distraction, GPU/paint cost, unsupported effects, forced-colors failure, and ignoring reduced-transparency preferences.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** Transparent cards over a gradient are not enough. Glass should clarify layer hierarchy; a solid fallback is part of the material contract.
- **Translation:** Limit glass to a named layer role, place text and data on contrast-bounded internal surfaces, test every background/state, and provide opaque and reduced-transparency fallbacks before adding decorative blur.
- **Foundations:** [Color](../foundations/color-system.md) · [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Motion](../foundations/motion-interaction.md) · [Spacing](../foundations/spacing.md). **Evidence:** UX2, SYS1.

### Neumorphism

- **Aliases · category:** Soft UI, neumorphic design · interface material style.
- **Core · boundary:** Controls and surfaces appear softly extruded from a shared background through paired light and dark shading. Unlike Skeuomorphism, it usually lacks a specific real-world object model; unlike Glassmorphism, it is opaque and relief-based.
- **Defining cues:** Near-monochrome surface family; paired highlight/shadow relief; rounded continuous forms; low apparent edge contrast.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits bounded experimental or decorative surfaces after evidence; likely mismatches primary controls, forms, dense dashboards, and low-vision contexts.
- **Risks:** Inherent contrast pressure, weak clickability, unclear pressed/default state, expensive shadow rendering, and forced-color collapse.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** Soft shadows and rounded cards alone are not a reason to adopt it. Its characteristic low contrast is a known liability, not a requirement to preserve.
- **Translation:** Treat relief as a secondary material cue only; restore explicit borders, text contrast, focus, and redundant state cues, and reject the style if those corrections erase the concept or still fail acceptance criteria.
- **Foundations:** [Color](../foundations/color-system.md) · [Icons](../foundations/iconography.md) · [Motion](../foundations/motion-interaction.md) · [Spacing](../foundations/spacing.md). **Evidence:** UX1; WCAG remains normative.

### Web Brutalism

- **Aliases · category:** Brutalist web design, raw-web brutalism; qualify "web" when architecture is a plausible reading · digital vernacular/interface paradigm.
- **Core · boundary:** Exposes the web's native structure and production means through direct HTML-like hierarchy, limited finish, and deliberate resistance to polished convention. Unlike Neobrutalism, it is usually raw, spare, and system-like rather than colorful, bordered, and shadowed.
- **Defining cues:** Browser- or system-adjacent typography; visible links and document hierarchy; sparse material treatment; abrupt scale or spacing; dense or stark composition with little cosmetic mediation.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits art, portfolios, archives, indie publishing, and products whose candor is credible; mismatches trust-sensitive commerce or broad public services when anti-polish reduces comprehension.
- **Risks:** Mistaking brokenness for honesty, poor responsive behavior, inaccessible defaults, hostile tone, and fragile browser inconsistency.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** An unstyled page is not automatically brutalist. Native structure must be deliberately composed and fully functional.
- **Translation:** Make document structure, links, and system status visually explicit; remove cosmetic mediation only where it improves directness, while preserving responsive layout, readable type, complete states, and robust controls.
- **Foundations:** [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Content](../foundations/content-writing.md) · [Motion](../foundations/motion-interaction.md). **Evidence:** UX2.

### Neobrutalism

- **Aliases · category:** Neubrutalism, neobrutalist web design · contemporary web vernacular.
- **Core · boundary:** Graphic, engineered rawness using conspicuous borders, hard offset depth, bold blocks, and playful high contrast. Unlike Web Brutalism, it is typically orderly, colorful, and intentionally illustrated rather than close to browser defaults.
- **Defining cues:** Thick visible boundaries; hard single-color offset shadows; blocky geometry; bold display type; bright or strongly separated color roles; limited rounding.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits creator tools, youth brands, campaigns, and products needing memorable directness; mismatches solemn or dense workflows when every element competes.
- **Risks:** Visual fatigue, thick boundaries consuming small screens, loud color combinations, shadow/state confusion, and novelty masking generic structure.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** A black border and offset shadow do not make the direction. Preserve hierarchy and whitespace; do not call raw HTML neobrutalism.
- **Translation:** Give only hierarchy-bearing containers a conspicuous border/depth rule, pair expressive display type with a quiet reading role, and keep state, focus, target, and contrast cues semantically distinct.
- **Foundations:** [Color](../foundations/color-system.md) · [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Spacing](../foundations/spacing.md). **Evidence:** UX2.

### Editorial Design

- **Aliases · category:** Editorial, publication design; archival is a compatible sub-register, not an alias for every editorial direction · stable professional visual vernacular.
- **Core · boundary:** Content is paced through typographic hierarchy, columns, image sequence, captions, rules, and recurring publication rhythm. Unlike Swiss Style, voice and narrative pacing may be expressive; unlike a news product category, the grammar can apply beyond publishing.
- **Defining cues:** Deliberate display/body/metadata roles; column and measure variation; captions, folios, rules, or annotations; image sequencing; rhythmic alternation between dense and open passages.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits museums, archives, media, portfolios, reports, and story-rich commerce; mismatches high-frequency controls when page-like pacing obscures direct manipulation.
- **Risks:** Fragile responsive composition, long-content overflow, licensed type cost, inaccessible pull quotes, and print metaphors that break interaction.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** A serif heading and large photograph do not make editorial design. Metadata, reading sequence, and content rhythm must form one system.
- **Translation:** Define publication-like roles for title, body, metadata, caption, and media; vary measure and pacing around real content while keeping navigation, controls, and responsive source order predictable.
- **Foundations:** [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Layout](../foundations/grid-layout.md) · [Imagery](../foundations/imagery.md) · [Content](../foundations/content-writing.md). **Evidence:** ED1, SG1.

### Early Web

- **Aliases · category:** Early-web vernacular, GeoCities-era web; "retro web" is broader and should name a period · historical digital vernacular.
- **Core · boundary:** The visible, personal, document-like web before widespread professional design-system convergence. Unlike Web Brutalism, its rawness often comes from period tools and amateur authorship rather than an anti-polish doctrine; unlike Y2K, it is not defined by glossy millennial futurism.
- **Defining cues:** System and bitmap-era type; conspicuous links and browser metaphors; tiled or low-resolution imagery; compact hand-built composition; visible badges, counters, separators, or page furniture.
- **Fit · mismatch:** Fits archives, personal sites, games, music, and historically grounded community experiences; mismatches essential services or transactions when period conventions reduce comprehension.
- **Risks:** Flashing or auto-playing media, illegible patterns, nonresponsive layouts, image text, inaccessible widgets, and slow nostalgic asset piles.
- **Anti-caricature · false friends:** Pixel rendering, marquees, and "under construction" badges are techniques. Preserve the era's personal authorship and visible web structure without recreating its failures.
- **Translation:** Choose one documented period convention for navigation, type, or imagery and pair it with a modern semantic/responsive core; keep motion user-controlled and every nostalgic asset purposeful and licensed.
- **Foundations:** [Type](../foundations/typography.md) · [Imagery](../foundations/imagery.md) · [Motion](../foundations/motion-interaction.md) · [Content](../foundations/content-writing.md). **Evidence:** EW1.

## 5. Reclassified vocabulary

These terms remain useful, but the inclusion gate does not support a standalone card in this version.

| Term | Disposition | How to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Modernism** | Broader historical family | Clarify whether the decision calls for Bauhaus, Swiss Style, Minimalism, a postwar register, or simply current design. "Modern" often means contemporary rather than Modernism. |
| **Mid-century Modern** | Period/design modifier | Name the actual postwar graphic, product, or architectural source and translate only its evidenced traits. The retrospective label spans too many practices for one interface card in this version. Evidence: MM1. |
| **Postmodernism** | Broader historical family | Select Memphis or New Wave Typography when their grammar fits; otherwise translate the intended quotation, plurality, irony, or rule-breaking directly. |
| **Brutalism** | Ambiguous family | Qualify Web Brutalism, Neobrutalism, or architecture. Architectural concrete is context, not an interface recipe. |
| **Utilitarian** | Broader direction family | Translate task efficiency, explicit labels/states, robust type, visible structure, and low ornament through the foundations; do not fake austerity. |
| **Industrial** | Material/tonal modifier | Add exposed structure, mechanical rhythm, or raw material cues to one anchor; distinguish from Web Brutalism and utilitarian task design. |
| **Soft / material** | Material/tonal modifier | Name rounded, tactile, diffuse, or layered traits. Capitalized **Material Design** routes to Google's governed system. Softness never excuses low contrast. |
| **Restrained minimal** | Anchor + modifier | Use Minimalism as the anchor and restrained as the tone; specify what is removed and where identity remains. |
| **Kinetic / expressive** | Motion/tonal modifier | Name the purpose, exposure, and intensity through Motion & Interaction; it is not a static visual style. |
| **Playful** | Tonal modifier | Translate to bounded shape, copy, illustration, or motion decisions suited to audience and task; preserve seriousness where the flow requires it. |
| **Archival** | Content/production family | Usually pair Editorial Design with an archival modifier: catalog metadata, provenance, plates, records, and collection navigation. Do not simulate age when the archive is digital-born. |
| **Technical / diagrammatic** | Information family | Translate annotation, schematic relationships, tabular data, and instrumentation through typography, layout, imagery, and content. Diagramming is not decoration. |
| **Luxury / premium** | Brand/register modifier | Require brand, audience, price, material, and trust evidence; translate restraint, craft, service, or rarity rather than assuming dark color, serif type, or metallic accents. |
| **Retro** | Period modifier | Name the period and source; "retro" alone is too broad to choose a grammar. Early Web is an anchor only when that documented vernacular is intended. |
| **Y2K** | Period modifier | Name the relevant 1990s/early-2000s techno-optimist cues and keep them subordinate to an anchor; avoid treating all chrome or translucency as Y2K. |
| **Vaporwave** | Cultural/tonal modifier | Specify ironic corporate nostalgia, collage, color, or media texture; verify that the register fits the product and avoid generic neon nostalgia. |
| **Cyberpunk** | Genre/tonal modifier | Specify high-tech/low-life tension, information density, terminal cues, or controlled accent contrast. Avoid pseudo-Asian signage and other cultural shorthand. |
| **Retrofuturism** | Period/genre modifier | Name whose past imagined which future and select observable period geometry, type, imagery, or material cues; avoid generic space-age costume. |
| **Platform-native** | Governed strategy | Follow the target platform's current official design language and adopted implementation before aesthetic exploration. |

**Why 21 anchors:** the retained set has a stable name, reliable semantic support, distinctions on at least three relevant dimensions, useful interface scope, and a viable accessible translation. The reclassified terms are broader families, moods, periods, materials, genres, or tones whose names alone underdetermine a full interface direction. No additional anchors were added merely to fill the range.

## 6. Techniques and patterns

Gradients, bento layouts, pixel rendering, collage, filigree, blur, grain, halftone, hard shadows, split layouts, and similar devices are techniques or patterns. Select them only after an anchor and modifier have established why they belong. State the device's information or identity job, affected region, responsive behavior, asset/runtime cost, and removal condition. A technique never supplies audience, task, hierarchy, or accessibility evidence.

## 7. Branded and governed systems

A governed system is a structural contract, not an aesthetic shorthand. Inspect the project's adopted version, platform, packages, tokens, and components; use the current official documentation; clarify version only when it cannot be inferred and would change the result. Official component behavior, accessibility, platform semantics, and migration guidance take precedence over a style card. Mix systems only through an explicit compatibility seam.

| Governed request | Official source of truth | Route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Material Design / Material 3** | [Google Material Design 3](https://m3.material.io/) | Determine platform and adopted Material version; use official foundations, components, motion, and implementation. Do not reinterpret "Material" as generic softness. |
| **Fluent / Fluent 2** | [Microsoft Fluent 2](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/) | Follow the target platform package and Fluent contracts; Acrylic or Mica is one governed material, not permission to glass every surface. |
| **Apple platform language** | [Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/) | Select the actual Apple platform and current HIG; native behavior and accessibility settings govern materials, controls, navigation, and motion. |
| **Carbon** | [IBM Carbon Design System](https://carbondesignsystem.com/) | Use the adopted Carbon assets and contribution/version rules; technical appearance alone is not Carbon. |
| **GOV.UK Design System** | [GOV.UK Design System](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/) | Treat service patterns, content, accessibility, and tested components as contracts rather than a utilitarian look. |
| **U.S. Web Design System** | [USWDS](https://designsystem.digital.gov/) | Follow the applicable government requirements, maturity guidance, components, and tokens; do not copy only its visual surface. |

## 8. Ambiguity and cultural care

- Clarify only when the answer changes the design: **modern** (current or Modernism), **brutalist** (architecture, Web Brutalism, or Neobrutalism), **material** (physical quality or Material Design), **New Wave** (typography or another cultural domain), and **retro** (which period and place) are common forks.
- Name the historical or cultural source when material, then translate it into observable type, composition, color-role, material, imagery, density, or motion decisions. Prefer evidence over aesthetic cosplay.
- Keep ideology, propaganda, sacred forms, scripts, national symbols, and marginalized identities out of decorative shorthand. Constructivism, Futurism, cyberpunk, and other loaded labels require especially clear boundaries.
- Never use pseudo-script, generalized "ethnic" motifs, or a culture's people and places as texture. Ask for the specific reference or replace the label with observable traits.
- Preserve real brand and subject provenance. A named style may organize supplied evidence; it does not manufacture authenticity.

**Completion:** one evidence-backed primary anchor and at most one compatible modifier have been translated into observable foundation decisions; any combination has a conceptual seam and conflict rule; branded/platform and accessibility contracts win; false friends, mismatches, and implementation risks are explicit; the result still serves the Design Read.
